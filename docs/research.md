# Business hypotheses and research direction

Status: hypotheses as of September 17, 2026; no market validation or customer commitments claimed.

## Proposed differentiation

Regional screening for repeat users may be more valuable than exhaustive analysis of a single municipality. Test one building category across several municipalities within an actual customer's delivery/service area.

Possible buyers include prefab manufacturers, repeat builders, development acquisition teams, and planning consultants. Compare model-first territory scouting with address-first qualification of incoming customer enquiries. Do not assume the preferred workflow before observing real work.

The proposed durable asset is maintained, source-backed regulatory data and correction history. Its economics depend on several customers reusing the same coverage. Housing need, public-sector support, and unused factory capacity do not independently prove willingness to pay for zoning software.

## Research reports

1. **Regional scanning competition:** distinguish address lookup, selected-parcel feasibility, multi-municipality scouting, reverse model search, and data APIs. Verify coverage depth and customer use, not just vendor labels. Gridics demonstrates the category; Canadian coverage remains unconfirmed in this review. Its public MuniMap deployments need not represent all product coverage.
2. **Prefab workflows and competitors:** inspect manufacturer selectors, ADU tools, land/home marketplaces, standardized plans, and sales tools. Separate lead collection from actual zoning evaluation and distinguish modular, panelized, manufactured, and standardized designs.
3. **Factory utilization and bottlenecks:** verify practical versus announced capacity and actual output. Compare zoning/site discovery with financing, working capital, committed orders, delivery cost, site readiness, and procurement as explanations for underutilization.
4. **Government demand and substitution:** distinguish announcements, operational programs, awarded contracts, and completed projects. Identify actual buyers/budgets and whether public tools complement or replace the proposed service.
5. **Independent synthesis:** choose one customer/workflow/geography or recommend waiting. Use transparent scenarios and a small validation plan, not speculative market-size claims.

For every report, separate verified facts, vendor claims, inference, and unknowns. Include dates and primary-source links, disconfirming evidence, alternative explanations, and questions requiring interviews. Missing public evidence does not establish absence. Desk research does not establish willingness to pay.

## Inexpensive validation

- Interview repeat users about recent real projects, lost opportunities, existing tools, effort, and purchase authority.
- Offer a paid, manually reviewed shortlist for two designs and a batch of real sites; measure repeat demand and net investigation time saved.
- Compare the original extraction prompt and a minimally revised version against roughly 40–60 annotated clauses. Measure total accepted-rule cost, including human correction, against manual curation.
- After the Vancouver technical demonstration, compare three municipalities in one customer's real territory. Measure marginal ingestion/update cost and added customer usefulness.
- Replay a verified amendment and inspect changed rules and affected decisions.

Initial proposed pilot gates include no false exclusions in the small reviewed in-scope sample, approximately 30% net investigation-time savings, and repeated paid use. These are experimental targets, not validated performance guarantees or statistically sufficient production assurance.
