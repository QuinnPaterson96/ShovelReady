# Victoria permit records pilot

A homeowner deciding where to place a building can take a specific file and a question to a discovery call. In the preliminary property scan, **Permit records to review** uses the selected Victoria parcel’s link to the City’s development application history. A mapped application near a property alone does not establish that link.

## Source check on November 19, 2026

The City parcel layer returned parcel 6632 for GISLINK 02130017. Its history included six files for 450 Dallas Road. Five were development or variance permit files in the pilot’s scope. The sixth was a rezoning application, which remains visible in application history. Two illustrate why the file status alone cannot establish issuance.

| [DDP01187](https://tender.victoria.ca/webapps/ourcity/Prospero/Details.aspx?folderNumber=DDP01187) | The tracker displays an issuance task with a blank completion date. The parcel history lists the application as active. | Is there a later decision or drawing for the proposed change? |

The parcel history is a live observation. Tracker details for these two files are dated observations. Issuance and the availability of an approved drawing do not establish a condition applicable to a new project. That requires the relevant clause or sheet, revision, amendments and a match to the proposed work. All three final-approved links returned PDF responses from the City on November 19. Their filenames identify architectural, landscape and civil plans. The pilot links to the City-hosted files. ## Validation still needed

Before expanding to Saanich, product research can ask a planner to compare the current checklist and this pilot on ten residential Victoria parcels, including multi-address, amended and no-match cases. The proposed threshold is a more specific, actionable discovery question on at least three parcels with zero wrong-parcel or false-issuance claims. This requires a planner and remains open. If a decision depends on private records, the owner and planner can obtain the issued instrument and relevant amendments with the required consent. Homeowner research can separately test whether people can find the file and use the question on a discovery call.
