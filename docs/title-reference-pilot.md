# BC title reference pilot

A homeowner preparing a builder enquiry may already have a title entry, registered instrument or survey plan. The useful next step is a question about the proposed work. For example, “Does the right-of-way plan cover the proposed building location, and who can check that before we settle on a layout?”

## Two ways to prepare a review request

Open **Title restrictions** on the selected mapped BC property. **Get my BC title** points to Explorer in another tab. For an unambiguous supported parcel match, copy the mapped nine-digit PID and compare it with an independent property record before paying. For a missing or ambiguous PID, confirm the parcel separately. The homeowner chooses the title and pays LTSA directly. Referenced instruments and plans are separate purchases. The PDF is delivered through LTSA. Keep the assessment tab open and return to **I already have my title**. [Explorer ordering help](https://help.ltsa.ca/ltsa-explorer/search-titles-and-plans) · [Download instructions](https://help.ltsa.ca/ltsa-explorer/download-items) · [Current fees](https://ltsa.ca/fees/)

With a document in hand, add the number, date and question you want to share. Use the review handoff to preview the parcel and question for a qualified reviewer. You can copy or download the draft and, when a placement is available for the identified parcel, preview and save its sketch separately. The homeowner chooses a recipient and sends the material. The builder enquiry uses the same owner-supplied reference. Email sharing respects the existing property-details control. Changing properties clears the title reference.

For direct return of title PDFs inside ShovelReady, see the [feasibility decision and provider questions](bc-title-direct-feasibility.md).

## Evidence behind the pilot

Victoria’s Special Restrictions layer exposes an object identifier and point geometry. A mapped point can prompt a follow-up. The layer supplies neither the registered instrument nor the area it covers. An empty map search leaves the title question open. [City layer and fields](https://maps.victoria.ca/server/rest/services/OpenData/OpenData_PlanningAndDevelopment/MapServer/1)

LTSA’s public training example shows why the number and question belong together. Several rights of way can reference different portions of the same plan. An owner can bring the number from a document already in hand. If further investigation is warranted, LTSA supports document and plan searches by number. [LTSA worked example](https://ltsa.ca/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/2022_10_Fundamentals-of-LT-Applications.pdf) · [LTSA document and plan searches](https://help.ltsa.ca/ltsa-explorer/search-documents-and-plans)

## Before expanding

Product research should compare the existing checklist with the reference-and-question handoff on ten consented Victoria and Saanich cases. Include a map miss with a registered interest and a cancelled entry. Ask a qualified reviewer to identify the next document or professional. The proposed expansion threshold is at least eight correct next steps, an improvement over the existing card, and zero unsupported clearance or prohibition calls. Include homeowner and builder feedback on what they intended to share and what they would do next.

That study requires recruitment, permission to use the case records, and a qualified reviewer. Software fixtures exercise the handoff. For a specific site, a BC real-estate solicitor can assess the legal effect and a BC land surveyor can locate the affected area relative to proposed work. Any future registry integration or hosted document reuse needs separate access and reuse review. [LTSA customer agreement](https://help.ltsa.ca/ltsa-customer-agreement)
